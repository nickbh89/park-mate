Supermarket car park time limits in the UK

Most supermarket car parks are free, but only for a set time. Overstay and a camera-issued charge of up to £100 can follow. Here is what the limits usually are and why you still have to read the sign.

Updated 15 October 2026 · UK private car parks · Written by BH Apps, makers of ParkMate

Typical limits by supermarket

There is no national rule. Each store sets its own limit with the parking company that manages the site, so two branches of the same chain a few miles apart can have different limits. These are the ranges that come up most often in the UK:

Treat these as a starting point, not a promise. The sign at the entrance and on the poles inside the car park is the only thing that counts, and it is what the operator will rely on if you appeal.

How the limit is enforced

Almost all supermarket car parks now use Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR). A camera photographs your plate on the way in and again on the way out, and the operator's system works out how long you were there. If that is longer than the free period plus the grace period, a Parking Charge Notice is posted to the registered keeper, usually within two weeks.

There is no attendant and no ticket on the windscreen, which is why people are often surprised when the letter arrives. The limit also runs from when you drive in, not from when you park up or walk into the store.

Two periods are built in by the industry code of practice: a consideration period of at least five minutes at the start, so you can read the sign and leave if you do not agree with the terms, and a grace period of at least ten minutes at the end. Operators do not have to tell you about either, and they do not stack up into a free extra quarter of an hour you can rely on.

Why people get caught

  • Two shops in one trip. A retail park with a supermarket, a coffee shop and a clothes shop can easily take longer than 90 minutes.
  • Click and collect queues. Waiting counts the same as shopping.
  • Staying in the car. Eating lunch, taking a call or waiting for someone all run down the clock.
  • Leaving and coming back. Many sites have a no-return rule, often one or two hours, and treat a second visit inside that window as one long stay.
  • Not a customer. Some sites are free for customers only, and parking there to go elsewhere is a breach from the first minute.

How to avoid a charge

Read the sign when you arrive. Note the limit, whether it is customers only, and whether there is a no-return rule. If you are going to be longer than the limit, move the car or pay for the extra time where a tariff exists.

Common questions

How long can you park at Aldi?

Usually 90 minutes, though some stores allow 60 or 120. The limit is on the sign at the entrance and inside the car park, and it applies from the moment the ANPR camera records you driving in.

How long can you park at Lidl?

Typically 90 minutes for customers. Some larger stores allow longer. Lidl car parks are for customers only, so parking there to go elsewhere can lead to a charge even inside the time limit.

How long can you park at Tesco?

Commonly two to three hours, but it varies by store format. Tesco Express and Metro car parks are often shorter than Superstore and Extra sites. Check the sign.

Is there a grace period at supermarket car parks?

Yes. The industry code of practice requires at least a ten-minute grace period after the free time ends, and a five-minute consideration period at the start. They are a safety margin, not extra free parking you can plan around.

What happens if I overstay?

The operator posts a Parking Charge Notice to the registered keeper, usually within 14 days. In England and Wales the charge is capped at £100 under the industry code, reduced to £60 if paid within 14 days. If you believe it is wrong, appeal to the operator first, then to the independent appeals service named on the notice.

This guide is general information about private parking in the UK, not legal advice. Terms vary from car park to car park, and the signs on site are the binding record. If you have received a charge, check the operator's own appeals process and the independent appeals services linked above.
