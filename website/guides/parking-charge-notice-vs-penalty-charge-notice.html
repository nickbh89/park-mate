Parking Charge Notice vs Penalty Charge Notice

They share the initials PCN, they arrive in similar envelopes, and they are not the same thing at all. Knowing which one you have changes who you deal with, how much you can owe and how to appeal.

Penalty Charge Notice

A Penalty Charge Notice is issued by a local authority under the Traffic Management Act 2004 (or by Transport for London). It covers parking on public roads, in council-run car parks, and things like bus lanes and box junctions. It is a civil penalty set by law, not a bill from a business, and the council has statutory powers to enforce it.

You can make informal representations to the council, then a formal appeal, and finally take it to an independent adjudicator (the Traffic Penalty Tribunal, or London Tribunals in the capital).

Parking Charge Notice

A Parking Charge Notice comes from a private company managing private land. It is not a fine, whatever the letter looks like. By driving in and staying, the operator says you accepted the terms on the signs, and the charge is their claim for breaking those terms, for example by overstaying, not being a customer, or parking outside a bay.

Operators get your name and address from the DVLA, which is why the letter reaches the registered keeper. In England and Wales the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012 lets them pursue the keeper if the driver is not named. In Scotland and Northern Ireland that keeper liability does not apply, which is one reason the process plays out differently there.

Operators are members of either the British Parking Association (BPA) or the International Parking Community (IPC). Since October 2024 both work to a single code of practice that sets the £100 cap, the 14-day discount, the five-minute consideration period and the ten-minute grace period.

What to do with each

  • Check which one it is. Council notices say Penalty Charge Notice and name the council. Private ones say Parking Charge Notice and name a company.
  • Do not ignore either. Both get more expensive and harder to challenge with time.
  • Appeal if there are grounds. Unclear signs, a genuine short overstay inside the grace period, a broken payment machine, or a notice sent late are all common grounds for a private charge.
  • Keep evidence. Photos of the signs, receipts showing you were a customer, and your own timings.

Common questions

Is a Parking Charge Notice a fine?

No. Only councils and the police can issue fines and penalties. A Parking Charge Notice is a private company's claim that you broke the terms on the sign. It can still be enforced through the county court if ignored, so it should be dealt with.

Do I have to pay a private parking charge?

You should either pay it or appeal it. If you ignore it, the operator can send debt recovery letters and ultimately issue a county court claim. A reasonable appeal with evidence often succeeds, especially for short overstays or unclear signage.

How much is a private parking charge in the UK?

Under the industry code of practice in force since October 2024, up to £100 in England and Wales, reduced to £60 if paid within 14 days. Some sites with a stated higher tariff differ, and Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own arrangements.

Can I appeal to POPLA or the IAS straight away?

No. You appeal to the operator first, normally within 28 days. If they reject it they must give you a code for the independent service: POPLA for BPA members, the IAS for IPC members.

This guide is general information about private parking in the UK, not legal advice. Terms vary from car park to car park, and the signs on site are the binding record. If you have received a charge, check the operator's own appeals process and the independent appeals services linked above.
