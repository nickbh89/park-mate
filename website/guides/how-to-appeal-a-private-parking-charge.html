How to appeal a private parking charge

A private Parking Charge Notice is a claim, not a fine, and a clear appeal with evidence succeeds more often than people expect. Here is the process in order.

Updated 15 October 2026 · UK private car parks · Written by BH Apps, makers of ParkMate

Step 1: check the notice

Confirm it is a Parking Charge Notice from a private company, not a Penalty Charge Notice from a council (the process for those is different). Check the date it was sent, the car park, the stated breach and the times. If it arrived more than 14 days after the event, the operator may have lost the right to pursue the keeper under the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012 in England and Wales.

Step 2: decide on your grounds

  • Short overstay. Less than ten minutes past the limit is inside the grace period.
  • Signs were unclear, missing or unlit. Photograph them at the same time of day.
  • You were a customer. Receipts or bank statements showing a purchase in store.
  • Payment machine or app failed. Screenshots, error messages, bank records of the attempt.
  • You left and came back. ANPR sometimes merges two short visits into one long stay. Receipts from elsewhere help.
  • Mitigating circumstances. Breakdown, medical emergency, helping someone. Operators must consider these under the code of practice.

Step 3: appeal to the operator

You normally have 28 days. Do it online via the link on the notice or by post, keep it short and factual, attach your evidence, and keep a copy. Do not pay first, because paying closes the matter. The discounted rate is usually frozen while your appeal is considered, and the operator must tell you the outcome.

You do not have to say who was driving. If you were not the driver, you can say so, but in England and Wales the keeper can still be held liable if the notice was issued correctly.

Step 4: go to the independent appeals service

If the operator rejects your appeal they must give you a code for the independent service: POPLA for British Parking Association members, or the IAS for International Parking Community members. You usually have 28 days from the rejection. It is free, done online, and the operator cannot add to the charge while it runs. Set out the same grounds, respond to anything in the operator's rejection, and upload all your evidence.

Step 5: if it goes further

If the independent appeal fails you can still choose not to pay, but the operator can then issue a county court claim. At that point take advice; the Citizens Advice website has a clear guide. Most people settle at the discounted rate before it gets there, which is why winning at step 3 or 4 matters.

Common questions

How long do I have to appeal a parking charge notice?

Normally 28 days from the date on the notice for the first appeal to the operator, then 28 days from any rejection to go to POPLA or the IAS. The 14-day discount is usually held while the operator considers your appeal.

Should I pay the reduced amount or appeal?

If you have real grounds, appeal. Paying ends the matter and you cannot get the money back. If you were plainly at fault and have no grounds, the 14-day discounted rate is the cheapest outcome.

Can a private parking company take me to court?

Yes. A Parking Charge Notice is a contractual claim and the operator can issue a county court claim if it is unpaid and unappealed. They cannot use bailiffs without first winning a judgment.

Do private parking charges affect my credit score?

Not unless the operator wins a county court judgment and it goes unpaid for more than 30 days. Letters from debt recovery firms on their own do not affect your credit file.

This guide is general information about private parking in the UK, not legal advice. Terms vary from car park to car park, and the signs on site are the binding record. If you have received a charge, check the operator's own appeals process and the independent appeals services linked above.
