How long is the grace period in a private car park?

Ten minutes at the end and five minutes at the start, under the industry code of practice. But the clock does not work the way most people think.

Updated 15 October 2026 · UK private car parks · Written by BH Apps, makers of ParkMate

The two periods

  • Consideration period: at least 5 minutes at the start. This is time to find a space, read the sign and decide whether to stay. If you leave within it, no charge should be issued.
  • Grace period: at least 10 minutes at the end. Once your paid or free time expires, the operator must allow at least ten minutes before issuing a charge.

Both come from the single code of practice that the British Parking Association and the International Parking Community have applied to their members since October 2024. Operators who want access to DVLA keeper data have to follow it, so it covers nearly every ANPR-managed car park in the UK.

How it works with ANPR

ANPR measures the time between your plate entering and leaving. A 90-minute free stay with a ten-minute grace period means a charge can be issued at 100 minutes and one second. The consideration period is intended for people who leave straight away, not an extra five minutes on top of a stay, so you should not think of it as 105 minutes.

The camera does not know when you parked, walked to the shop or came back to the car. Queueing at the exit counts. Waiting for a space counts. Sitting in the car counts.

What the grace period is not

It is not something to plan around. Operators are allowed to issue a charge the moment it expires, and timestamps from the cameras are hard to argue with. It is a margin for the unexpected: a slow till, a trolley return, a child who needs the toilet.

It also does not apply to a no-return rule, to customers-only sites if you were not a customer, or to parking outside a marked bay. Those are breaches from the start.

Using it in an appeal

If your charge shows an overstay of less than ten minutes past the stated limit, that is a strong ground for appeal to the operator and then to POPLA or the IAS. Quote the code of practice, give the times from the notice, and keep it factual.

Common questions

Is there a 10-minute grace period at all car parks?

At nearly all private car parks run by BPA or IPC members, yes, because the single code of practice requires it. Council car parks have their own rules under the statutory guidance, which also sets a ten-minute grace period for paid parking in England.

Does the grace period apply to free car parks?

Yes. The ten-minute grace period applies after a free maximum stay as well as after paid time. The five-minute consideration period applies on arrival at any site.

Can I add the consideration period and the grace period together?

Not reliably. The consideration period is for drivers who look at the terms and leave. Once you have stayed, the operator treats the free time plus the ten-minute grace period as the limit.

I was 8 minutes over. Can I appeal?

Yes. An overstay shorter than the ten-minute grace period should not have been issued. Appeal to the operator first with the times from the notice, then to POPLA or the IAS if they reject it.

This guide is general information about private parking in the UK, not legal advice. Terms vary from car park to car park, and the signs on site are the binding record. If you have received a charge, check the operator's own appeals process and the independent appeals services linked above.
